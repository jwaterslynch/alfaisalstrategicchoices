Strategic vs Non-Strategic Responses

What makes a response "strategic"? Strategy involves tradeoffs — choosing to do X instead of Y.

The Strategy Test

A good strategy tells you what to say no to. If your "strategy" doesn't help you reject opportunities or allocate scarce resources, it's not a strategy — it's a wish list.

Simple test: Could a competitor say the exact same thing? If yes, it's not a source of differentiation.

✅ Strategic Responses

"Small by design, exceptional by nature"

↳ Choosing to stay small = rejecting growth

"Compete on depth not scale"

↳ Explicit tradeoff: depth OVER scale

"Curated for employers, chosen by students"

↳ Prioritizing employer relationships

❌ Non-Strategic Responses

"Faith, Knowledge, and Work"

↳ Who would advocate for the opposite?

"Students are priority"

↳ Every university says this

"Priority in education, a priority in excellence"

↳ Excellence is table stakes, not strategy

A Formula for Strategic Statements

"We will [specific action] instead of [alternative] because [strategic rationale]"

Example: "We will maintain small class sizes instead of expanding enrollment because personalized education is our core differentiator."
